Two cards per word

Every word makes a recognition card and a production card. The second one is the point of the deck: a definition written entirely in Lithuanian asks you to produce the word, which is why no definition ever contains the word it defines.

What's on a card

  • Audio on everything — the headword, its inflected forms, the Lithuanian definition and the example sentence. Four recordings per word.
  • Stress marks (kirtis) on headwords and on the inflected forms shown, not just the dictionary form.
  • Inflected forms on every card: nouns with the plural, verbs with 3rd person present and past.
  • Masculine and feminine, labelled: adjectives show both forms marked vyr. g. and mot. g., so the feminine is never read as a plural, and nouns with a form for each gender show the pair (mókytojas, mókytoja).
  • Full case tables for the personal pronouns and demonstratives, voiced — including the feminine column that most decks leave out.
  • The target word is bolded in the English translation, so it is obvious which word the sentence is teaching.
  • Tags for everything — tema::, pos::, batch:: — so you can build a filtered deck of just the verbs, or just the food theme. A key:: tag names each card's word (key::vardas), handy when reporting a mistake.

Getting it

Download the .apkg and open it with Anki, or import it from within Anki: File → Import. It arrives as Lietuvių A2 with a subdeck per theme. Importing a newer version over it updates the cards in place. Earlier versions are on the releases page.

Studying for recognition only? Suspend the second card type — it roughly halves the workload, and it is much the harder half.

How it was made

Honestly, and with the seams showing:

  • The word list follows Pusiaukelė, the official A2 content description (Ramonienė et al., 2006, revised 2016), which the State Commission of the Lithuanian Language publishes free so that teaching materials can be built on it. Its twelve topics are the first twelve themes, and about half of the headwords appear in its word index. The selection, definitions and examples are this deck's own.
  • Inflected forms and stress marks come from English Wiktionary, the VDU Kirčiuoklis stress tool and the phonology_engine library, cross-checked against each other. Every form is verified against the hunspell lt_LT dictionary.
  • Definitions, examples and translations were written with AI assistance and checked by automated rules — no headword inside its own definition, an inflected form in every example, restricted vocabulary, spell-checked throughout. They have not been proofread by a native speaker.
  • The audio is synthesised, not recorded: the Lithuanian synthesiser operated by UAB Intelektika, voice “astra”, built on the publicly funded LIEPA project.

Intelektika's terms of use state that “Mūsų suteiktų Paslaugų rezultatus galite naudoti tiek asmeniniais, tiek komerciniais tikslais” — the results of the service may be used for personal and commercial purposes. No attribution is required; the credit below is courtesy.

Report a mistake

Corrections are very welcome, above all from native speakers. If a definition, example, translation, form, stress mark or recording is wrong, report it with the word and what is wrong, and the fix goes into the next version.

The form is on GitHub and needs a free account. Reports are public, so you can see what is already reported.
